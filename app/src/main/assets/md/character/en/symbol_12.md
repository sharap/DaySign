The Mayan word EB literally means "staircase." Among the Quiché Maya the same sign is called Evob – "teeth" – and among the Aztecs it has two names at once: Itlan, meaning "teeth," and Malinalli, meaning "a bunch of grass, a broom." Although among the Yucatec Maya the main meaning of the word is still "staircase," its glyph too depicts a tooth surrounded by black dots – a conventional sign for a row of teeth (in Mayan pictograms a ring of dots around an object conveys the plural).

At first all these images – a staircase, teeth, a bunch of grass – seem entirely unconnected, but they are united by a common idea: each of them symbolically expresses the notions of togetherness, unity and orderliness.

Overall, the Staircase sign embodies the idea of orderliness, organization and communal life.
