The Maya word ETZNAB literally means "spearpoint", though other readings are also possible: "sharp edge", "splinter". By tradition this sign is interpreted as the Flint: it was from flint that the Indians made most of their weapons, including spearheads. In Indian culture flint is assigned roughly the same symbolic role that iron or steel plays for us. The Aztecs put the same meaning into this sign; they called it Tecpatl, which likewise means Flint.

The Etznab glyph is a stylized depiction of a split stone: two intersecting cracks. Sometimes it is framed by the outline of a knife or a spearhead, but the main and unchanging element remains the crack itself. It follows that the key meaning of the sign is not flint as such, but above all its sharp splinter.

So Etznab, or the Flint, is the sign of strength, firmness, sharpness and independence.
