A person born on a Reed day has no talent for being satisfied with what he has already achieved. The words "enough" and "everything in moderation" are simply missing from his vocabulary, and "better" he often takes to mean "more". That is how nature made him: what matters most to him is the very idea of growth and multiplication in everything he puts his hand to.

One of the surest ways to recognize a Reed person is his incredible persistence in pursuing a goal. For him, obstacles do not exist: he will press forward even if it means arguing with the whole world, and no thorns on the road will stop him. Just as grass, in its unstoppable growth, breaks through a thick layer of concrete and asphalt, a person born on this day is used to getting what he wants, whatever difficulties stand in his way.

Such qualities can only be envied. Patience and sheer driving force allow the Reed to build a brilliant career in almost any field, but for him, more than for anyone else, it is vital to choose the right road from the very start. Once this person has settled on a direction, he does not know how to retreat, and if the choice turns out to be wrong, he risks spending his whole life in fruitless attempts to, as the saying goes, break through a wall with his forehead, a wall that cannot be broken. With the same fervor with which he rushes toward his goal, he is capable of clinging to his own delusions.

It would be wrong, however, to claim that flexibility is entirely foreign to the Reed. Rather the opposite: he can be remarkably inventive, finding detours and loopholes where others do not even see them. People of this sign often have a very agile mind, and at times a cunning one. The Reed's consistency and constancy lie in the fact that all this ingenuity unfolds within one general course, which he has chosen for himself and from which he has no intention of turning aside.

A character that combines stubbornness with a lack of any sense of proportion often prevents the Reed person from stopping in time. On top of that, he is gifted with a rare talent for making a mountain out of a molehill. A trivial disagreement that someone else would not even notice can grow, in his case, into a deep grudge. Those around him often have no idea what is brewing inside him until one day the accumulated feelings gather strength and burst out. By then, settling the misunderstanding will be very difficult.

For this reason, when dealing with a Reed it is best to leave nothing unsaid and always to remember that his thoughts and feelings are astonishingly durable. He forgets nothing and is capable of keeping even a petty grievance in his memory to the end of his days.

Then again, exactly the same loyalty of feeling is his in good things too. He understands better than anyone what true friendship is worth, and he knows how to be a sincere and devoted friend.

Thanks to these traits, people born on this day become good professionals. Their horizons and views may not always be broad, but in their own field they know practically everything. More than that, they tirelessly accumulate professional knowledge and polish their skills, growing into specialists of a truly high caliber.

The Reed, like few others, knows how to immerse himself completely in his work without being distracted by trifles, and he never abandons what he has started halfway through. All these traits, together with patience, diligence and a readiness to work not only well but also hard, undoubtedly lead him to success. Such people quite often make themselves in the most literal sense, rising from the very bottom to the top.

Another hallmark of the Reed is keen observation combined with concreteness. He notices what escapes the attention of others and knows how to put it to use, turning a trifle into something important and valuable. It is a great virtue of his that he cannot stand ambiguity and vague wording, and he expresses his own thoughts with the utmost clarity and precision. Even where everything seems shaky and blurred and nothing can be pinned down exactly, the Reed finds the "solid ground" and the specifics he needs.

A vivid confirmation of this remarkable ability is the fact that both great founders of psychoanalysis, Sigmund Freud and Carl Gustav Jung, were born on precisely this day. And indeed, these men managed to bring clear logic into a domain where confusion and utter chaos had reigned before.

Still, the main thing that defines the Reed throughout his life is a craving for growth in one form or another, and most often he devotes his life to a professional career. If for some reason advancement at work proves impossible (which usually happens with women), he may direct the same aspirations toward children. Somewhere deep down the Reed intuitively feels a kinship with them: in children he sees the same thirst for unrestrained growth that he sees in himself.

In his attachment to children, as in everything else, he often knows no limits. The famous children's author Korney Chukovsky, for instance, who was born on this day, did not stop at writing fairy tales and poems for children: his house in Peredelkino became, in effect, a kindergarten, where holidays and games were organized for all the neighborhood kids.

The essential property of the Reed is that it amplifies many times over all of a person's qualities, both strengths and weaknesses. That is why it is so important for the Reed to choose truly worthy goals and channel his energy, which knows no barriers, in a constructive direction. Then he will be able to grow through any asphalt and concrete and, perhaps, reach the very stars.
