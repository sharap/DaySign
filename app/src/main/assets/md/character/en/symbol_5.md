The name CHICCHAN is rendered three ways at once — "clear sign", "sign of the snake", "sign of the sky". The reason lies in the root "chan" (today "kan"), behind which the Maya set three equally valid senses: "snake", "heavens" and "to see". That is why the Snake became so beloved an image among the Indians: through it they spoke of Divine, or Higher, vision.

The central figure of the glyph is a mat. Authority among the Maya was marked by it, much as ours is by a crown or a throne. Where the mat sits matters: it always takes the upper corner, and so the power meant is heavenly rather than earthly. Beside it a small snake winds, and the direction of its movement is deliberately left unclear — it either comes down from under the mat toward the earth or climbs from the earth upward. The drawing ties two worlds together: sky to earth, man to God.

Chicchan is that tie — what joins a person to the higher powers.
