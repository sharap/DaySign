As far as its name goes, the Earthquake is one of the most ambiguous signs. In interpreting it, the Maya invariably relied on a play on words: in the Maya language the root "kab" means both "earth" and "honey", so the word for "earthquake" can also be read as "honeyed". This sign also goes by another name: Incense.

On the symbolic level, the sign conveys the idea of a sensory upheaval, a state in which a person is overflowing with sensations.

The main element of the glyph is a stylized vessel with a drop of honey trickling out from under its lid. The vessel itself is usually drawn in the form of the graphic sign for honey (a wavy, curved line) and at the same time resembles the outline of a human face turned in profile. The lid, a straight horizontal stroke, denotes the uppermost limit in the language of Maya imagery.

In most cases the glyph of the sign Caban also includes a closed eye; from below, a vertical chain of black dots or dashes reaches up toward it, which is how the idea of gradual filling is conveyed. The fact that the eye is specifically closed emphasizes that the person is overflowing with thoughts, feelings or fantasies.

In other words, the Earthquake is the sign of strong feelings that overwhelm a person.
