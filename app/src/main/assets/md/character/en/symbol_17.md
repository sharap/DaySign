The name of this sign is trickier than the rest. The Maya played on the root "kab", which means both "earth" and "honey" — so the word for "earthquake" reads equally as "honeyed". Hence the sign's second name: Incense.

The meaning of the sign is an upheaval of feeling, the state in which sensation itself fills a person to the brim.

At the middle of the glyph stands a vessel with a drop of honey running out from under its lid. The vessel is usually drawn with the sign for honey, a wavy line, and its outline comes out like a face in profile. The lid — a straight horizontal stroke — means to the Maya the limit above which there is nothing.

Most often a closed eye belongs to the glyph as well, with a vertical row of black dots rising toward it from below: something is filling little by little. The eye is shut for a reason — the person is full from within, with thoughts, or feelings, or fantasies.

Caban is the sign of feeling that spills over the edge.
