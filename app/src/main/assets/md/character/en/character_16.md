The Vulture is a person with a sensual intellect. Nature has not shortchanged him in brains, but dry, dispassionate reasoning is completely foreign to him. His feelings are so deep and strong that they inevitably color his every thought. It can be said with confidence that a cool-headed analyst is definitely not him.

This depth of feeling inherent in the Vulture shows in every side of his life without exception. On the one hand, by his very nature he gravitates toward sensual pleasures: he loves good food and does not shy away from earthly joys and bodily delights. On the other hand, he tends to think seriously about moral and ethical questions. Reflections on good and evil, on right and wrong, on truth and lies may occupy the Vulture from his earliest childhood. In other words, his mind is not that of a dry scholar but rather that of a poet with a burning heart.

Another noticeable trait of the Vulture is directness, which those around him often mistake for excessive harshness of judgment. In short, a person of this sign does not suffer from a surplus of diplomacy - if anything, he suffers from a lack of it.

Vultures are indeed often intolerant. They react sharply to everything that seems wrong or unnecessary to them, yet they are not prone to hasty conclusions, much less to sweeping judgments. On the contrary, Nature has endowed them with a meticulous, probing mind. In any undertaking, in every question, they absolutely must get to the very heart of the matter, sorting the situation out piece by piece. That is why the Vulture's opinion on any subject is usually well founded and extremely hard to dispute.

In this respect the Vulture has the makings of a true researcher, and were his feelings not so deep, he would make an ideal scientist, able to see the very essence of things. And indeed, among the famous people born on this day there are many great minds. It is enough to mention James Watt, inventor of the first steam engine. From this brilliant invention grew, in effect, our entire civilization of machines: without it, our cars today would probably still be pulled by a team of horses.

Because of their emotionality, however, Vultures more often choose the road of the poet rather than the scientist, and creative natures are abundant among them. Self-examination and a desire to understand their own soul are typical of them, which is why the theme of justice that must be fought for, and of the search for truth, sounds so often in their lives and their work.

In general, turning to moral questions is very characteristic of the Vulture, yet the hypocrisy of which others suspect him is entirely absent from his nature. Of course, with his high sensuality the Vulture lives anything but a monastic life, and his behavior is far from always angelic, but that he sincerely strives for moral purity all the same is an indisputable fact.

Curiously, the Vulture's craving for purity sometimes makes itself known even at the level of the body. Somewhere deep down he often carries an unconscious aversion to dirt in any form. Dirt irritates him and throws him off balance. Sometimes it goes so far that he is ready to step into the shower several times a day and still feel not quite clean enough. That is just how he is made.

The Vulture may react the same way to anything he considers superfluous or obstructive. The Maya glyph of this sign depicts honey being pressed out of a honeycomb - an image of the Vulture's drive to get to the essence by discarding everything unnecessary. In this light it is hardly surprising that the famous phrase "Brevity is the sister of talent" belongs to the Russian classic Anton Chekhov, who was born precisely on the day of the Vulture.

Another manifestation of his meticulousness and persistence is a dislike of unresolved questions. The Vulture strives at any cost to clarify any situation, any problem, and to sort it out to the end.

In everyday life this often turns into having things out with the people around him, and above all with those closest to him. This trait can wear out those who live alongside the Vulture, but believe me, it is far better than hushing up problems and waiting until the accumulated discontent reaches boiling point. That is why, when dealing with the Vulture, it is better not to skirt around the sharp corners but to examine the state of affairs without unnecessary emotion and work out a joint solution. The Vulture knows how to forgive; the only thing you will never get from him is a renunciation of his own ideas of justice.

Naturally, the Vulture's character can hardly be called easy or convenient to live with, and the extraordinary depth of his feelings is to blame for that. In return, Nature has given him great inner strength, so the Vulture has every opportunity, having passed through the thorns and cast aside the superfluous, to reach the sweet honey after all and achieve considerable success in life.
