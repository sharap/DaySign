The Maya word BEN goes back to a root meaning "to go", and in sense it corresponds to the Russian word "pobeg", which means both "escape" and "shoot" – here in the botanical meaning of a sprout or offshoot. The Ben sign embodies the idea of purposeful growth; not for nothing does the "Book of the Omens of the Days" list "putting forth shoots" among the attributes of this day.

Among the Aztecs and the K'iche' Maya this sign is called Reed, which in the language of images means the same thing: the main feature of a reed is precisely its intense growth in a single direction. Whereas a tree develops not only upward but also outward, a reed stretches in one direction only.

The Ben glyph schematically depicts reeds growing in several rows, one row above another. The lower row of stalks rests against a double horizontal line, while the upper row rises above it. This image carries the idea of hyper-intensive growth, of outgrowing that recognizes no frames or boundaries.

In other words, Ben is the sign of purposeful movement toward something, the sign of unceasing and steady growth.
