The Maya word MULUC literally means "a supply of water" or simply "a supply"; it is built on the root "mul" – "to gather" – and "uc" – "drink." The Aztec counterpart of this sign is Atl, "water." Both the Yucatec Maya and the Aztecs lived in arid lands, so water was among their most vital assets: without reserves of it, survival would have been simply impossible.

The central element of the Muluc glyph is a drop in the form of a small circle in the middle. In Maya texts this sign denoted not only water but supplies in general. Sometimes the Muluc glyph – for example, in the Madrid Codex – also shows a symbolic open palm holding the same drop-circle. The image as a whole expresses the idea of gathering, of receiving something.

In a broader sense, this sign speaks of the hidden reserves that the body sets aside for critical situations.
