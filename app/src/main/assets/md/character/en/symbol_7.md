The word MANIK in the Maya language literally means "acquiring the spirit" or, turned around, "the spirit of acquisition": the root "man" translates as "to acquire", "to buy", and "ik" as "spirit, wind, desire". Among the K'iche' Maya the same sign is called Deer, and in their tradition the deer has from time immemorial been a symbol everyone understood – the symbol of the hunter's prize. So the name Deer carries the very same idea as the words "acquisition, gain".

The glyph of the Manik sign shows an open palm with a small circle in the middle – an image of receiving something. Above it is inscribed, upside down, the sign for "spirit, wind, life", which is drawn as a T-shaped cross. The fact that the sign is inverted indicates that what is meant is not the Heavenly Spirit as such, but the desires and passions that Nature has planted in a person and that drive him.

Thus the Deer sign embodies human desires themselves and the craving to fulfill them.
