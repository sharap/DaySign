The word CIMI in the Maya language literally means "the one who has died". However, it should not be forgotten that the Indians regarded death quite differently from Europeans. It is enough to recall that in Latin America the Day of the Dead remains one of the best-loved holidays to this day.

For the Maya, the Skull sign is a sign of trial. It embodies not so much death in the usual sense of the word as strength, will, firmness and endurance as traits of character. Therefore, to avoid a mistaken interpretation of the meaning contained in it, in translation we settled on one of its possible meanings – the Skull as an image of the sturdiness of the human body.

The Cimi glyph is a stylized representation of a human skull, whose obligatory detail is a set of sharp, strong teeth. Often the symbol of power – the mat – is inscribed inside the skull.

So the Skull stands for endurance and calm inner strength.
