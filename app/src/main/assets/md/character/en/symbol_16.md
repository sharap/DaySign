Vulture is a name that came from the K'iche' and the Aztecs. To the Maya themselves the sign is CIB, "wax", and the word grew out of the root "kib" — "cleansing". The ancient Maya had no use for wax at all and treated it as waste. Candles were poured from it only after the Europeans arrived; the earlier sense was forgotten by then, and "kib" settled onto the wax candle. From that period comes a line in the "Book of the Order of Days": "On the day Cib the Lord created the first candle, and light appeared where there was neither sun nor moon."

The glyph draws a honeycomb with the honey being pressed out of it; the pressing itself is carried by the spiral at the centre. The thought behind it is a plain one: take what is needed and discard the rest.

Cib is about cleansing and clarity, in whatever sense one cares to give those words.
