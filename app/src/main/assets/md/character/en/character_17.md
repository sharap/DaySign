A person born on the day of the Earthquake is a dreamer and a fantasist by his very nature. He gladly takes from life everything it has to offer: he is far from indifferent to beautiful objects, good food, well-made clothes and attractive faces of the opposite sex. But there is a curious detail: to feel happy, he does not need to own any of it at all. The mere thought that in principle he could have it is enough to send him into a state close to euphoria. Put simply, dreams and fantasy give the Earthquake person no less, and often more, than their fulfillment.

Many women behave in much the same way when they go shopping not for any particular purchase but, as the saying goes, to "feast their eyes". People born under the Earthquake sign often do exactly the same, and, it must be admitted, they are entirely right, for they possess the chief secret of the human soul: if you want to be happy, be happy.

And it is true: you can own everything in the world and remain miserable, or have almost nothing and yet be happy. It all comes down to the intensity of one's experiences, and in a person born on the day of the Earthquake the sensations and fantasies are so vivid that they can fill him completely.

People of this sign love to travel, and they do not need exotic countries for that: the most ordinary outing into nature brings them full and genuine joy. Here, too, the main thing is to "feast the eyes", and their rich imagination will paint in all the rest, and then some.

This gift of the Earthquake person, the ability to stay happy in any situation, is enviable, but there is one catch: someone who is already content lacks the incentive to move toward a goal. The knack for being satisfied with little often becomes the very thing that keeps people of this sign from rising truly high in life. Indeed, why reach for the stars, and through thorns at that, when everything is fine as it is? It is far easier to go for a walk in the nearest country park and daydream, and from such a simple pastime the Earthquake person will get just as much pleasure, if not more.

This explains a pattern that seems odd at first glance: among those born on this day you will hardly find anyone who has become famous in politics, sports or business. They are dreamers, not fighters. It does happen, of course, that life itself carries them to the top, but they put little effort of their own into getting there.

The careers of Earthquake people turn out best when their powerful imagination finds an outlet in creative work: there, their emotional depth and their gift for delighting in simple things are in demand and can prove contagious. A telling example is the great director Alfred Hitchcock, who was born on this day. In his films he never tackled the weightiest themes of human life, yet he knew how to enchant the viewer with the sheer force of an unbridled imagination, infinitely removed from everyday reality.

This sign has another flip side as well. Those born on this day rejoice wholeheartedly in small things, but small troubles can sometimes wound them too deeply, throw them off balance and stir up a whole storm of negative emotions. Nature, however, has also provided protection against unnecessary distress: that same dreaminess and imagination allow the Earthquake person simply to "close his eyes" to what is unpleasant and turn his gaze to something more agreeable. And since he finds something agreeable practically everywhere, this comes to him without effort.

It is important to remember, though, that the "closed eyes" tactic works only as long as the troubles really are small. When a serious problem is brewing, turning away from it is simply dangerous. Such behavior can lead, and as a rule does lead, to a gradual buildup of negative tension inside, which sooner or later is bound to break out, and then the whole thing threatens to end in a real explosion.

From this point of view it becomes clear why the Maya gave this day the name of the Earthquake. Deep in the Earth, enormous tectonic energy quietly accumulates; for a time it gives no sign of itself, and then it suddenly bursts forth and levels entire cities. In just the same way, for a representative of this sign, running away from genuinely solving his problems can turn into the gravest of consequences.

This is the central paradox of the Earthquake: people under its influence tend to overlook obvious problems, are easily carried away by fantastic ideas and disregard reality until life itself, sometimes very painfully, reminds them that you cannot hide from problems, you have to solve them. Fortunately, the Earthquake person is quite up to such a task. The Maya book "Omens of the Days" calls these people "very quick-witted": they have a lively, agile mind and are perfectly capable of dealing with their difficulties before those lead to serious consequences. And in every other respect, the gift of sincerely rejoicing in and savoring the simplest things gives people of this sign every chance of living a long and, most importantly, happy life.
