The first thing that strikes you about a person born under the Staircase sign is how collected he is. He belongs to that uncommon breed of people who, having set out to go fishing, would never leave the rods at home. This collectedness shows itself literally everywhere: a person born on this day reasons consistently, cannot stand pointless running about, and his convictions are usually firm and rarely change. His belongings may not be in immaculate order, but he can still tell you at any moment exactly where everything is.

In our disorderly world such qualities can hardly be called superfluous. What is more, it is precisely thanks to his organization that the Staircase person often achieves success in the most varied fields. He is a born organizer and leader: he knows how not only to give orders but also to follow the rules himself. Not for nothing is it said that only someone who knows what it is to carry out another's orders can command well. Small wonder, then, that this day gave the world the two most celebrated Soviet commanders of the Second World War – Marshals Georgy Zhukov and Konstantin Rokossovsky, whose troops took Berlin, the capital of Nazi Germany, in 1945.

The clear mind and logic with which the Staircase is endowed help him build a career in many directions. Yet no one could call him a dry stick: he has feelings enough, and he is capable of finding fulfilment even where a creative streak is required. The paradox is that the depth of his emotions does nothing to prevent him from reasoning soberly and taking a situation apart piece by piece.

Compassion and responsiveness are things the Staircase person knows first-hand. At times he takes not only his own troubles but other people's too much to heart. Even if on the outside he seems stern and unbending, there is no doubt that a very gentle soul hides behind that mask – it is just that trials and painful experiences have hardened his character to the point of toughness.

The Staircase is a good family man. As a rule, people of this sign put the interests of those close to them above their own: selfishness is foreign to their nature. At times they can be too strict with their household, but behind that strictness there is always sincere love and care.

The Staircase has one more distinctive feature: no one believes in justice the way a person born on this day does. Nature has arranged things so that his head is dominated by reflections on how a properly ordered world ought to look.

Musings on good and evil, on right and wrong, on how people ought to behave – himself included – are highly characteristic of the Staircase. It is no accident that this sign gave the world Leo Tolstoy, the greatest classic of world literature, and no accident that questions of morality stand at the centre of every one of his books. It also says a great deal that Tolstoy set his peasants free before serfdom was abolished – and was left with almost nothing.

Altogether, the Staircase is the most "socialist" sign, in the best sense of the word. Yet the Staircase's craving for justice is by no means always easy to put into practice – and not at all because people reject just laws, but because everyone understands justice in his own way. Morality touches the feelings, and where feelings are at work, looking for logic is often useless. That is why the Staircase's attempts to bring order to a realm where passions run high and everyone has his own notion of good and evil are most often doomed to failure. This can complicate his life considerably and fray his nerves.

Here lies the main danger of the sign: not for nothing, as everyone knows, is the road to hell paved with good intentions. The Staircase person's excessive desire to remake the world for the better, and the obstacles he meets along that road, can irritate both him and those around him. On this slippery slope it is easy to slide into empty moralizing. So as not to poison life for himself or for others, a person born on the day of the Staircase should simply follow the chief commandment of the Gospel – accept people as they are, and judge not. Love changes the world for the better far more powerfully than any morality.

In other words, it would do the Staircase person no harm to soften and balance somehow his heightened demands – those he makes of himself and of others alike. If he learns to look indulgently on small human weaknesses, he has every chance of finding the harmony he needs with the world around him and living long, happy years.

The influence of the Staircase sign on people is very strong. Do not be surprised if someone born on this day is drawn to questions of politics or religion; whatever the case, his organization, logic, clarity of mind and craving for order will help him rise to the heights he deserves. His fate may take the most varied paths, but one thing is certain: he will not squander his life on trifles.
