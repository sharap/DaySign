"Grain" is the conventional name of this sign. Books on the Maya calendar often use its Aztec name as well – "Lizard."

The Maya word KAN literally means "yellow," "ripe," "golden." The key idea of the Kan sign is ripening, and among the Maya its symbol was precisely a grain of maize. The sign embodies wealth in all its forms; it is no accident that in the indigenous texts the word "kan" is often used in the sense of "precious."

The Kan glyph itself figuratively depicts a grain buried in the earth. It often also includes a human eye watching over the grain. In all likelihood, a Maya play on words is hidden here: "kan" means "ripe," while "chan" (or "kan") means "to look"; in many ancient Maya words the sound "ch" shifts to "k." Curiously, a similar play on words can be found in Russian, where the verb "zret'" means both "to ripen" and "to look" or "to observe."

So the Kan sign – Grain – embodies the idea of ripening, fertility, wealth and coming abundance.
