The World Tree sign is sometimes also called the "Crocodile" – that is its name among the Aztecs. The Maya word IMIX literally means "a woman's breast": in Maya, "im" is "breast" and "ix" is "female". Most often, however, the word denotes the World Tree – the symbol of the birth of life and of nature's life-giving forces. It is no accident that the books of Chilam Balam symbolically depict the beginning of an era as the sprouting of the Imix Tree.

The same word is commonly applied to the ceiba, the tree sacred to the Maya: Indian shamans ascribe miraculous properties to its seeds and, as a rule, use them for divination.

The main element of the Maya glyph Imix schematically represents the nipple of a woman's breast, while the row of short vertical strokes in its lower part signifies the beginning of growth. Sometimes these marks are enclosed in a frame resembling a flower; frames as such carry no meaning in Maya glyphs, but here the border simply underscores the sign's overall meaning.

In sum, Imix – the World Tree – symbolizes the beginning of beginnings, conception, the hidden wellspring of life.
