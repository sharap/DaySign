The Maya word CAUAC literally means "it will tip over, it will overturn"; the word is formed from the verb "kawal". By tradition, however, this sign is interpreted as the Thunderstorm. The reason lies both in the character of the sign itself and in a play on words: "cauac" sounds similar to the Maya "k'a-wak'", meaning "a loud rumble, a crack, thunder".

The main element of the Cauac glyph is an upside-down mound built up of separate small circles. As a rule, it is closed off at the top by a straight horizontal line that denotes the upper limit. The combination of the sign for the upper limit with the overturned mound should be read as overflowing, a violent spilling over the edge. Another detail often found in the Cauac glyph is a slanted cross made of two short straight lines; it stands for a crack, a split, a breakthrough.

The glyph of the Cauac sign is frequently stylized as a human face in profile. Where the mouth should be, a grain symbol (a rectangle divided in half) is often placed, surrounded by a multitude of black dots. In the language of Maya imagery this reads as a great deal of talk and reflection about wealth and abundance.

So the Thunderstorm is the sign of feelings that overflow their banks, the sign of an emotional explosion.
