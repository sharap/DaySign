Eagle is the conventional name of this Maya sign, inherited from the Aztecs. The K'iche' Maya call it Tz'ikin, which means "bird". The Maya name proper, MEN, literally translates as "deed" or "occupation", and also as "talent, ability".

The glyph of the sign Men usually depicts a face - either an eagle's or a human one - with a chain of black dots running from its eyes to the brain. In the system of Maya pictograms such an image is read as "many-eyed" and "many-wise", that is, it points to keen observation and an exceptional mind. Often a straight horizontal line is drawn across the upper part of the head on the glyph; in pictograms it usually denotes the highest level, the upper boundary of something. Together with "many-wisdom" this should be interpreted as an indication of the utmost intellectual abilities.

Thus the sign of the Eagle - Men - embodies the idea of the highest capabilities of the intellect.
