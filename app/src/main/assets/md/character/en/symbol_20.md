AHAU is "lord", though the word has several literal readings: "the one who begins", "the one who distributes", "the one who throws the enemy onto his back". All of them come out of the many-sided "ah-hau". Among the Aztecs the sign was Xochitl, "flower of the day", that is, the Sun; a third reading turns up as well — the Ancestors.

In the glyph one makes out the face of a hunter with a blowgun. The blowgun is drawn as a circle inscribed within a circle, and male strength and fertility stand behind it.

The glyph rests on something else, though: two small circles parted by a double vertical stroke. To the Maya this is the record of a division — of a harvest or of property, where the circle is a share and the double stroke is the split. And since the parts come out equal and the drawing symmetrical, justice and balance are added to the sign.

Ahau is power that divides fairly and holds its balance for that reason.
