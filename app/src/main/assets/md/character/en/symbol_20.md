The Maya word AHAU means "lord", and its literal meanings are "the one who begins", "the one who distributes" and also "the one who throws the enemy onto his back"; all of these go back to the many-sided Maya expression "ah-hau". The Aztecs called this sign Xochitl, that is, "flower of the day", in other words the Sun. Occasionally one also encounters another interpretation of its name: the Ancestors.

The glyph of the Ahau sign is the stylized face of a hunter armed with a blowgun. The blowgun itself is drawn as a small circle inscribed within a larger one, and it symbolically conveys the idea of male power and fertility.

The main element of the Ahau glyph is two small circles with a vertical double line running between them. In the language of Maya imagery, this combination of symbols stands for the division of a harvest or of property: the circle here denotes a share, and the double band denotes division. Beyond that, the symmetry of the glyph and its division into equal parts convey the notions of "justice" and "balance".

To sum up: Ahau, the Lord, is the sign of power, justice and strength that rests in balance.
